01 — The shape of it

One boss, ten reps, a hundred cars

The boss sees everything. Each rep sees their own bookings and the cars that are free — and nothing whatsoever about anyone else's business. That separation is the product, and it is enforced in the database rather than hidden in the interface.

Fleet
~100 cars · 20 models · 8 pricing categories, held as one shared pool. Any rep can book any free car in the company.
Locations
Each rep is stationed at one hotel and books for its guests — which is why a room number is taken. A rep covering another hotel books there too, and that booking stays visible to both of them.
Booking unit
A specific car, by plate. Availability is a per-plate calendar, not a count per category.
Volume
Up to roughly 200 pickups and drop-offs a day across the company at peak.
Windows
Pickups 08:30–11:30, drop-offs 18:00–21:00. Defaults, overridable per booking, override recorded.
Lifecycle
Booked → Out → Returned, plus cancelled and no-show.

The rule everything else defers to

Anything out of the ordinary — a fuel shortfall, new damage, a late return, a driver who fails the age check — is recorded by the rep and decided by the boss. Reps gather evidence. They never price a charge and never argue money with a guest.

02 — How a day is counted

Morning to night, not 24 hours

This is the single most misread rule in car rental software, so it is worth being exact. A rental is counted in calendar dates held, inclusive of both ends. Collected Monday morning and returned Wednesday evening is three days, and the guest keeps the car until 21:00 on Wednesday at no extra cost.

Early return
No refund — the booked duration is charged in full. But the car is freed immediately, so the remaining dates reopen and it can be re-let the same week.
Turnaround
None. A car back at 19:00 is bookable at 08:30 the next morning.
Back-to-back
A booking ending the 15th and one starting the 16th are legal on the same car. Both starting the 15th is refused by the database itself, not by a check that a bug could skip.

03 — Pricing

The boss types the numbers, the app never invents one

Prices are entered as finished totals for each duration, so the +€5 first day and every other quirk of the price list is already inside the figures. The app performs no arithmetic of its own beyond adding extra days to a long rental.

Periods
Four or so pricing periods per season, defined as date ranges the admin sets himself and can redraw every year. Nothing about months or season boundaries is fixed in the software.
Table
A total for each category × period × duration, for one to seven days.
Eight days or more
The seven-day total plus a per-extra-day rate, also set per category and period.
Crossing a period
The pickup date decides. A 30 July collection is priced at the July rate for the whole rental, even if it runs into August.
Discounts
None. The price is locked to the table. Only the boss can amend a price, and every amendment is logged.
Free of charge
Baby seats — infant, child and booster — and additional drivers. Recorded on the booking, never priced.

Deliberate safeguard

Editing a price table never rewrites an existing booking. Each booking stores which period priced it, so last month's revenue stays exactly what it was when the money changed hands.

04 — Who sees what

A rep sees a blocked date, and nothing behind it

When a rep looks at a car they have no booking on, they see occupied dates. Not the rep, not the hotel, not the guest, not the times, not the price, not the reason. A car in the garage for repairs and a colleague's rental look identical — which is exactly right, and is achieved by how the data is stored rather than by remembering to hide things.

05 — At the desk

Ten pickups and ten returns, one hand, phone in sunlight

The rep app opens on today: their pickups and returns in time order, each with a single large action. Everything else is one tap away.

Pickup

A sequence of single-purpose screens that survives being interrupted mid-way — because it will be.

  • Licence — camera captures front and back for every driver. The card is read automatically and the fields come back pre-filled and fully editable. Typing it in by hand always works, and a worn or unusual licence never blocks a pickup.
  • Eligibility — 21 and over for categories A and B, 23 and over for the rest, licence held at least a year, and valid past the return date. A failure stops the pickup and names the rule that failed. Only the boss can override, and the override is recorded.
  • Fuel — level out, on an eighths slider. Same level back on return.
  • Damage — tap the car diagram to mark what is already there, with a photo per mark if it matters. At return, new marks appear in a different colour and go straight to the boss.
  • Agreement — the bilingual contract is generated from the booking, the guest signs on the screen with a finger, and a copy is emailed if they want one.
  • Payment — amount, method, paid or owing. No deposit is taken.

Changes

Before the car goes out, a rep can edit or cancel their own booking freely. Afterwards it is locked, with one exception: they can extend it. If the same car is already booked later, the app offers a free car in the same category and the guest swaps vehicle rather than losing the extension.

06 — The boss's app

The day sheet, replaced

Movements
Any chosen day, every hotel: pickups then returns in time order, with car, guest, room and rep. Printable. This is the morning screen.
Fleet board
All hundred cars live — out, free, blocked, back today.
Exceptions
His inbox. Fuel shortfalls, damage with photos, late returns, no-shows, override requests. He sets the charge and closes the item.
Bookings
Every booking ever made, searchable by guest, plate, date, rep or hotel, editable at any stage. Every edit is logged permanently — who, what, before, after, when.
Reports
Revenue by day, month, rep and category, with CSV export. Kept deliberately plain.
Fleet & pricing
Add, archive and delete cars; block a car's dates for service with a reason only he can see; set the pricing periods and tables.

He stays signed in on his desktop and his phone at the same time — logging in on one never signs out the other.

07 — Platform & data

An Android app from the Play Store, built as one web app

The system is a web application wrapped as a native Android package and published to the Play Store, so it installs with a real icon and listing while remaining a single codebase the boss can also open on a laptop. Bug fixes go live the moment they deploy, with no store review queue in the middle of a season.

Devices
Android only, via the Play Store. Desktop browser for the boss.
Connection
Required. A form in progress is held safely if signal drops and sent when it returns, so nothing is lost mid-pickup.
Languages
Greek and English, switchable per person. The rental agreement always prints in both.
Notifications
Reps get a morning list and an evening reminder. The boss gets exceptions as they happen.
Licence images
Stored privately, never on a public address, and deleted automatically once the retention window the boss sets has passed — 24 months by default. Every deletion is logged.
Compliance
GDPR-compliant handling throughout, with a real privacy policy, terms page and cookie consent. Accessibility to WCAG 2.1 AA.

Where the security actually sits

The realistic risk is not an outside attacker — it is a signed-in rep with a curious browser. So the separation between reps is enforced inside the database on every single query, not by which buttons the app chooses to draw. A rep who edits a request by hand still gets nothing back.

08 — Build order

Foundations first, because everything visible rests on them

The order below is deliberate: availability and pricing are built and tested before a single screen, because a mistake in either one poisons everything downstream. If the schedule tightens, what gives is at the bottom of the list, never the top.

  1. PHASE 0Foundations — project, hosting, accounts, both languages, sign-in~3 days
  2. PHASE 1Data & engines — availability, pricing, the separation between reps, fleet import~1 week
  3. PHASE 2Booking core — availability screen, new booking, day sheet, fleet board~1 week
  4. PHASE 3Pickup & return — camera, eligibility, fuel, damage, exceptions, cash~1 week
  5. PHASE 4Contract & licence reading — bilingual PDF, on-screen signature, automatic licence read~1 week
  6. TESTPilot at one hotel, running alongside paper for a fortnight — paper is the safety net and the comparison is the testearly Oct 2026
  7. PHASE 5Refinement — act on what the reps actually did, then reports, notifications, Play Store listing, accessibility and speed audits, trainingOct – Feb
  8. LAUNCHLive, with two months of real running before the season opens1 Mar 2027

An honest word about October

"As close to release as possible" in five weeks is achievable for the operational core and tight for everything at once. The order above protects the parts that must be right — availability, the separation between reps, and the two flows the reps live in all day. Automatic licence reading degrades to typing, and reports can wait until there is real data to report on. Neither would spoil the pilot.
